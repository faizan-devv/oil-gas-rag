## 1. System Overview

### 1.1 Scope and Purpose

This reference manual provides a comprehensive overview of the five primary equipment families installed on the drilling rig: mud pumps, top drives, the BOP stack, shale shakers, and the drawworks. Its purpose is to serve as a technical resource for field engineers and maintenance technicians, enabling them to perform fault diagnosis, scheduled maintenance, and operational troubleshooting in accordance with established procedures. The manual is designed to complement the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) documentation, though in cases where discrepancies arise between this guide and the OEM manual, the OEM manual shall take precedence. Any such discrepancies must be promptly reported to the maintenance supervisor for review and resolution.

The manual is structured to facilitate efficient access to information relevant to each equipment family, ensuring that personnel can quickly locate procedures and specifications necessary for routine and emergency operations. It includes detailed descriptions of system functions, maintenance schedules, and fault codes, which are identified using the standard format FLT-NNNN. Equipment identification follows a consistent naming convention, with each component designated by the FAMILY-NN format, such as MUD-PUMP-04. This standardized approach ensures clarity and consistency across all documentation and communication related to the rig’s equipment. The manual is intended to be used in conjunction with OEM manuals and should not be considered a substitute for the detailed technical specifications provided by the original equipment manufacturers.

### 1.2 Circulating System

The circulating system is a critical component of the drilling rig, responsible for maintaining the continuous flow of drilling fluid, or mud, through the wellbore. This system ensures the removal of cuttings, control of downhole pressure, and cooling of the drill bit. The rig is equipped with four triplex mud pumps, designated MUD-PUMP-01 through MUD-PUMP-04, each with a rated power of 1600 horsepower. These pumps are capable of generating a maximum discharge pressure of 5000 psi and can operate at a maximum speed of 120 strokes per minute. During normal drilling operations, two or three of these pumps are typically in use, with the fourth maintained on standby to ensure operational continuity and redundancy.

Each mud pump is fitted with a pulsation dampener on the discharge side, pre-charged with nitrogen to 30 percent of the expected operating pressure. This dampener helps to reduce pressure fluctuations in the mud flow, which can otherwise cause wear on the system and affect the efficiency of the drilling process. After being circulated through the well, the mud returns to the surface and passes over the shale shakers, where cuttings are removed before the fluid is directed back to the active pits. This closed-loop system ensures that the drilling fluid remains in constant circulation, maintaining the necessary conditions for safe and effective drilling operations.

### 1.3 Hoisting and Rotary Systems

The hoisting and rotary systems of the drilling rig are integral to the safe and efficient operation of the rig. Central to this system is the drawworks, designated as DRAWWORKS-01, which is rated at 1500 horsepower. This unit provides the necessary power for lifting and lowering the drill string and is equipped with a disc brake as the primary braking mechanism. The disc brake ensures controlled deceleration and precise positioning of the drill string during operations. A drum encoder is integrated into the system to provide real-time block position data to the driller's console, enabling accurate monitoring and control. This data is also transmitted to the crown and floor saver system, which helps in preventing equipment damage during hoisting operations.

The rotary system is supported by two top drives, with TOP-DRIVE-01 currently in service and TOP-DRIVE-02 available as a spare. Each top drive is rated for a hoisting load of 500 tons and a continuous drilling torque of 50,000 foot-pounds. These units are essential for rotating the drill string and facilitating drilling operations at the bit. The top drive gearbox is oil-lubricated and is equipped with its own temperature sensor to monitor operating conditions and ensure proper lubrication. This feature helps in maintaining the longevity of the gearbox and prevents overheating, which could lead to mechanical failure. The combination of the drawworks, top drives, and associated systems ensures the rig can handle the demanding requirements of modern drilling operations.

### 1.4 Well Control and Solids Control

The well control system on the rig is designed to manage and contain formation pressures during drilling operations, ensuring the safety of personnel and equipment. A critical component of this system is the BOP-STACK-01, a 13-5/8 inch, 10000 psi stack equipped with one annular preventer and three ram preventers. This stack is essential for sealing the wellbore in the event of a kick or blowout, providing multiple layers of redundancy to maintain well integrity. The stack is hydraulically operated and integrated with the accumulator unit, which maintains system pressure at 3000 psi. The accumulator unit is further supported by nitrogen pre-charges of 1000 psi in each bottle, ensuring consistent and reliable operation even under demanding conditions.

Complementing the well control system is the solids control system, which manages the removal of drilled cuttings from the drilling fluid. This system includes three linear-motion shale shakers, designated as SHALE-SHAKER-01 to SHALE-SHAKER-03. These shakers are typically equipped with API 140 screens, which are designed to efficiently separate solids from the drilling fluid based on particle size. Each shaker is driven by two vibrator motors, which enhance the separation efficiency by creating a vibratory motion that facilitates the movement of cuttings across the screen surface. The solids control system is vital in maintaining the quality and properties of the drilling fluid, ensuring optimal drilling performance and minimizing environmental impact.

## 2. Component Specifications

### 2.1 Mud Pump Fluid End

The fluid end of the mud pump is a critical component responsible for the high-pressure delivery of drilling fluid to the wellbore. It is designed to accommodate liner sizes ranging from 5.5 inches to 7 inches, with the selected liner size determining the maximum allowable discharge pressure. Proper selection and installation of the liner are essential to ensure the pump operates within its specified pressure limits and maintains system integrity under operational stress. The fluid end incorporates piston seals constructed from nitrile rubber, which are rated to withstand temperatures up to 120 degrees Celsius. These seals are vital for maintaining the hydraulic seal between the piston and liner, preventing leakage and ensuring efficient operation under high-pressure conditions.

To maintain optimal performance and prevent overheating, the liner wash system must deliver a minimum of 40 litres per minute of coolant to each liner. This continuous flow of coolant is essential for dissipating heat generated during operation and prolonging the service life of the liner and associated components. The liner retainer nut is specified to be torqued to 1350 Nm, ensuring a secure and leak-proof connection. Additionally, valves and seats within the fluid end are designed to be replaced as matched pairs. This requirement ensures that the components work in harmony, maintaining the correct flow characteristics and preventing premature wear or failure due to mismatched parts. Adherence to these specifications is crucial for the safe and efficient operation of the mud pump.

### 2.2 Top Drive Gearbox and Bearings

The top drive gearbox is a critical component responsible for transmitting torque from the motor to the drill string. It is designed to operate with ISO VG 220 gear oil, which provides the necessary viscosity and lubrication properties under the operational conditions of the drilling environment. The system is capable of holding a total of 95 litres of oil, ensuring sufficient lubrication and heat dissipation during extended operations. To maintain optimal performance and prevent overheating, the gearbox is equipped with temperature monitoring systems. An alarm is triggered when the oil temperature reaches 85 degrees Celsius, indicating the need for immediate attention. If the temperature rises to 95 degrees Celsius, the system automatically shuts down to prevent damage to internal components.

In addition to temperature monitoring, the top drive system includes vibration sensors to assess the condition of the main bearing. Vibration levels are measured in RMS (root mean square) values, with an alarm threshold set at 7.1 mm/s. If the vibration exceeds 11 mm/s RMS, the unit must be shut down immediately to prevent further degradation or failure. The main bearing is separately grease-lubricated, distinct from the gearbox oil system, ensuring that each component receives the appropriate type of lubrication. This design helps to minimize contamination and ensures that both the gearbox and the bearing operate under optimal conditions. Regular maintenance and monitoring of these parameters are essential to ensure the reliability and longevity of the top drive system.

### 2.3 BOP Stack and Accumulator

The BOP stack is a critical component of the well control system, designed to seal the wellbore in the event of a kick or blowout. It consists of multiple ram preventers and an annular preventer, each with specific closure time requirements to ensure rapid and effective well control. The ram preventers must close within 30 seconds, while the annular preventer must achieve closure within 45 seconds. These response times are essential to minimize the risk of uncontrolled flow and ensure the safety of personnel and equipment. The stack is powered by hydraulic fluid stored in the accumulator, which must maintain sufficient pressure to operate all preventers even when the charge pumps are not in use. The accumulator must hold enough usable fluid to close all preventers and still retain a minimum pressure of 1200 psi, ensuring reliable operation during emergencies.

The annular sealing element is a consumable part that requires periodic inspection and replacement. It must be replaced when it fails to hold the required test pressure or exhibits visible cracking, both of which indicate degradation and potential failure. Regular testing is essential to verify the integrity of the sealing element and the overall functionality of the BOP stack. Testing procedures include a low-pressure test at 250 psi, held for 5 minutes, followed by a high-pressure test to confirm the system's ability to maintain pressure under operational conditions. These tests ensure that the BOP stack remains fully functional and ready to respond to any well control event. Proper maintenance and adherence to testing protocols are crucial to maintaining the reliability and performance of the BOP system.

### 2.4 Shale Shakers and Drawworks Brake

Shale shakers are critical components in the solids control system of a drilling rig, operating at a standard acceleration of 7.0 G to effectively separate cuttings from the drilling fluid. The deck angle of the shaker can be adjusted within a range of minus 1 to plus 5 degrees, allowing for optimization based on the specific drilling conditions and fluid properties. Each vibrator motor, responsible for generating the necessary vibration, is rated at 2.5 horsepower, ensuring sufficient energy for efficient solids removal. The screens used in the shaker system are tensioned to 50 Nm on each tension bolt, maintaining the structural integrity and performance of the screen assembly during operation.

The drawworks brake system is essential for controlling the movement of the drilling rig’s hoisting mechanism. Brake pads are manufactured to a standard thickness of 25 mm when new and must be replaced when they are reduced to 6 mm to ensure safe and effective braking performance. The drum encoder, which provides feedback on the rotational position of the drawworks drum, has a resolution of 1024 pulses per revolution, enabling precise control and monitoring of the hoisting operation. Proper maintenance of both the shale shakers and drawworks brake components is vital to ensure continuous and safe drilling operations, minimizing downtime and maximizing equipment efficiency.

### 3.2 Mud Pump Faults

Faults related to the mud pump can significantly impact drilling operations and must be diagnosed and resolved promptly. One common issue is a piston seal leak, identified by fault code FLT-0392. This condition is typically indicated by fluid weeping from the liner tell-tale hole and a fluctuation in discharge pressure exceeding 5 percent. To address this, the pump must be isolated and locked out, followed by bleeding the discharge pressure to zero and draining the fluid end. The liner retainer should then be removed, and the piston withdrawn. When replacing the seal, ensure the new seal is oriented with its lip facing the pressure side, and apply clean water as lubrication before reassembly. The liner retainer nut must be torqued to 1350 Nm to ensure proper sealing and mechanical integrity. After replacement, the pump should be run in at 40 strokes per minute for 15 minutes, and the tell-tale hole should be checked for any signs of leakage before resuming service.

Another frequent fault is liner wear, indicated by fault code FLT-0417. This is characterized by a gradual decrease in discharge pressure at a constant pump speed. To resolve this, the liner and its associated piston must be replaced together to restore performance. Additionally, fault code FLT-0455 relates to low dampener pre-charge, which results in increased vibration in the discharge line. This issue should be corrected by recharging the dampener with nitrogen, as using air or oxygen can lead to hazardous conditions. Proper maintenance of these components is essential to ensure the safe and efficient operation of the mud pump.

### 3.3 Top Drive Faults

Fault code FLT-0521 indicates a gearbox oil over-temperature condition, which is triggered when the oil temperature reaches 85 degrees Celsius. This fault should be addressed promptly to prevent damage to the gearbox components. Initial diagnostic steps involve verifying the oil level and inspecting the oil cooler fan for proper operation. If both are found to be within normal parameters, the next course of action is to drain the existing oil, replace it with fresh lubricant, and collect a sample for further analysis. This ensures that any potential contamination or degradation of the oil is identified and mitigated.

Fault code FLT-0538 is associated with high main bearing vibration, specifically when the vibration exceeds 7.1 mm/s RMS. This condition may indicate wear or misalignment within the bearing assembly. The recommended procedure is to re-grease the bearing and re-measure the vibration levels. If the vibration remains above the threshold after this intervention, the bearing must be replaced to prevent further mechanical failure. In cases where a top drive unit experiences FLT-0538 twice within a 24-hour period, it is necessary to replace the unit with the designated spare, TOP-DRIVE-02, to ensure operational continuity and safety. These measures are critical in maintaining the reliability and performance of the top drive system.

### 3.4 BOP Stack Faults

Fault diagnosis of the BOP stack is critical to ensuring the integrity and safety of drilling operations. One common fault is low accumulator pressure, identified by fault code FLT-0610, which is triggered when the system pressure drops below 2700 psi. This fault may indicate a malfunction in the charge pumps or the presence of hydraulic leaks within the system. To address FLT-0610, the charge pumps should be inspected and tested for proper operation. Additionally, the hydraulic system should be thoroughly checked for leaks, which can lead to a gradual loss of pressure. The accumulator bottles should also be evaluated, and any bottle with a pre-charge pressure below 900 psi must be recharged to restore system functionality and maintain safe operating conditions.

Another critical fault is annular preventer element leakage, indicated by FLT-0633. This fault is raised when the annular preventer fails to maintain test pressure, suggesting a compromised sealing element. The integrity of the annular preventer is essential for maintaining well control, and any leakage must be addressed immediately. The sealing element must be replaced before resuming drilling operations, as continuing to drill with an active FLT-0633 is strictly prohibited. All BOP-related faults, regardless of their severity or resolution status, must be promptly reported to the maintenance supervisor to ensure timely and appropriate corrective actions are taken. This ensures that potential risks are mitigated and operational safety is upheld.

### 3.5 Shale Shaker and Drawworks Faults

Faults related to the shale shaker and drawworks can significantly impact the efficiency and safety of drilling operations. One common issue is the occurrence of FLT-0704, which is typically identified by the presence of solids in the active pit or mud running off the end of the deck. This fault is caused by a torn or blinded screen, which compromises the solids control process. To resolve this, the affected screens must be replaced if torn or thoroughly washed if blinded. Ensuring the integrity of the shale shaker screens is crucial to maintaining proper mud properties and preventing unnecessary wear on downstream equipment.

Another critical fault is FLT-0719, which indicates a failure in one of the vibrator motors. Since both motors on a shaker are designed to operate in tandem for balance, a failure in one necessitates the replacement of both as a pair. This ensures consistent performance and prevents mechanical imbalance that could lead to further damage or operational inefficiencies. In the case of the drawworks, FLT-0802 signals excessive brake pad wear beyond the 6 mm limit. This fault requires immediate attention, as continued use of the drawworks for hoisting operations could lead to mechanical failure or safety hazards. Replacement of the brake pads is essential before resuming any hoisting activities.

Additionally, FLT-0815 involves a drum encoder signal fault, which can disrupt the accuracy of the drawworks’ operation. Initial troubleshooting should focus on inspecting the connector and cable for any damage or disconnection. If these components are intact, the encoder must be recalibrated against the block height reference marks to restore proper signal integrity and ensure precise control of the drawworks.

## 4. Maintenance Schedules

### 4.1 Per-Tour and Daily Checks

Per-tour checks are essential to ensure the operational integrity of key drilling rig components. During each tour, personnel must inspect the mud pump liner wash flow to confirm proper circulation and look for any signs of leakage through the tell-tale holes. This inspection helps identify potential internal wear or damage that could lead to pump failure. Additionally, shaker screens should be examined for tears or damage, as compromised screens can reduce solids control efficiency and affect drilling fluid performance. These checks are critical for maintaining the reliability of the mud system and preventing costly downtime.

Daily maintenance tasks include monitoring the top drive gearbox oil temperature and accumulator pressure, which are recorded in the maintenance log along with the equipment ID. These readings provide insight into the operational condition of the top drive system and help detect early signs of mechanical stress or hydraulic issues. On a daily basis, the gearbox oil level must be checked to ensure it remains within the specified range, and the top drive main bearing should be greased according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. Visual inspection of the drawworks brake pad thickness is also required to ensure safe and effective braking performance. All readings and observations are documented in the maintenance log to support predictive maintenance strategies and ensure compliance with safety and operational standards.

### 4.2 Weekly and Monthly Tasks

The BOP stack requires a function test every 7 days to ensure proper operation and readiness in emergency situations. This test is essential for verifying that all components of the blowout preventer system are functioning as intended. In addition, a pressure test is conducted every 21 days to confirm the integrity of the system under operational stress. These periodic checks help prevent potential failures and maintain the safety of the drilling operation.

Weekly maintenance includes inspecting the pulsation dampener pre-charge on every mud pump. This inspection ensures that the dampener is operating efficiently, which is critical for maintaining stable pump performance and reducing system wear. Additionally, the drawworks brake pad thickness must be measured using a gauge to ensure it remains within acceptable limits. This task is vital for ensuring the braking system can handle the required load and prevent overheating or failure during operation.

Monthly maintenance tasks involve taking an oil sample from the top drive gearbox for analysis. This helps detect early signs of wear or contamination, allowing for timely maintenance before significant damage occurs. Furthermore, the shaker motor mounting bolts should be checked to ensure they are secure and free from loosening, which could lead to equipment misalignment or failure. Lastly, the drum encoder calibration must be verified against the block height reference marks to ensure accurate measurement of the traveling block position, which is essential for proper hoisting operations.

### 4.3 Scheduled Replacement Intervals

Scheduled replacement intervals for critical components of the drilling rig are essential to maintaining operational efficiency and safety. Mud pump piston seals must be replaced every 500 operating hours or immediately upon the occurrence of fault FLT-0392, whichever occurs first. This ensures that the pump maintains optimal performance and prevents potential failures that could lead to downtime or equipment damage. In addition, mud pump liners are subject to replacement every 1500 operating hours. These intervals are based on wear characteristics and operational stress, ensuring that the pump continues to function within specified parameters without compromising drilling efficiency.

The top drive system requires regular maintenance to ensure reliable operation. The top drive gearbox oil must be changed every 1000 operating hours or every 6 months, whichever comes first, to maintain proper lubrication and prevent mechanical failure. Furthermore, the top drive main bearing is designed for long-term use and is scheduled for replacement every 8000 operating hours. This interval is determined by the expected wear life of the component under normal operating conditions. Lastly, annular sealing elements are replaced every 12 months regardless of their apparent condition. This proactive replacement schedule is implemented to prevent potential leaks or failures that could compromise well control and safety. Adherence to these intervals is crucial for maintaining the integrity and reliability of the drilling rig's critical systems.

### 4.4 Record Keeping and Escalation

All maintenance log entries must include the equipment ID, the fault code if applicable, the action taken, the engineer's name, and the date of the entry. This ensures a clear and traceable record of all maintenance activities, facilitating both immediate troubleshooting and long-term analysis of equipment performance. Each entry must be documented in the designated logbook or digital system, depending on the rig's current procedures, and must be verified by the responsible engineer prior to submission.

If a fault remains unresolved after 24 hours from the initial report, it must be escalated to the maintenance supervisor for further evaluation and action. This ensures that persistent issues are addressed promptly and do not compromise operational safety or efficiency. Additionally, any fault code that recurs three times on the same piece of equipment within a 30-day period must be escalated to the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) for further investigation. This escalation is critical for identifying potential design or component issues that may require manufacturer intervention or parts replacement.

The resolution status of each reported fault must be clearly indicated as resolved, pending, or escalated. This classification allows for effective tracking of maintenance progress and ensures that all relevant personnel are aware of the current status of each reported issue. Proper record keeping and timely escalation are essential components of a comprehensive maintenance strategy, ensuring that all equipment remains in optimal working condition and that potential problems are addressed before they lead to more severe operational disruptions.
